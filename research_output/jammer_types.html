GNSS Jammer Types — Evidence Map

Five jammer classes referenced in 04_paper_draft.md Section 5 (Table 1). Each class shown with animated spectral signature, threat profile, and mitigation evidence strength.

1. Jammer Class Gallery

CW Continuous Wave

Single sinusoidal carrier at one frequency. Simplest interference signature — narrow spectral spike, constant amplitude.
Spectrum
Single tone, ~zero bandwidth
Source
Unintentional emitters, lab signal generators
Best mitigation
Adaptive Notch Filter (ANF) — Strong evidence
L1C/E1 CBOC gain
3–5 dB over L1 C/A (Jafarnia-Jahromi 2015)
Why it matters

CW is benchmark case. ANF cancel single tone optimally. Most controlled-lab anti-jamming demos use CW because behavior tractable analytically.

NB Narrowband Multi-tone

Multiple closely spaced tones. Bandwidth small relative to GNSS spreading code (~2 MHz for L1 C/A).
Spectrum
Cluster of tones, < 500 kHz BW
Source
Spurious emissions, harmonics, intermod products
Best mitigation
FDAF, ANF (multi-notch) — Moderate evidence
Failure point
ANF order limit when tones > notches available
Why it matters

Real spurious emitters rarely pure CW. NB tests algorithm scaling under more than one interferer. Eigenspace decomp (Broumandan 2016) targets this case.

WB Wideband Gaussian

Flat-spectrum noise-like jammer spanning GNSS band. Worst-case noise-floor elevator. Drives high-power outages.
Spectrum
Flat across > 2 MHz
Source
High-power military-adjacent emitters, regional outages
Best mitigation
CRPA spatial nulling + INS bridging
INS limit
MEMS bridges only ~35 s (Lashley & Bevly 2015)
Why it matters

Source of Eastern Mediterranean / Baltic outages (Murrian 2021, Osechas 2022). No corpus study demonstrates full mitigation against WB at field level — biggest deployment gap.

Chirp Swept FM (PPD)

Linear frequency-modulated sweep across GNSS band. Dominant commercial threat — all 18 PPDs in Mitch 2011 use chirp.
Spectrum
Sweep, ~10 MHz BW, sub-ms period
Source
Personal Privacy Devices (PPD), $20–$50 USD
Best mitigation
Pulse blanking after band-limit — Strong
Mechanism
Front-end BW converts sweep → duty-cycled pulses
Blanking gain
10–15 dB J/N (Borio 2016)
Why it matters

Newark GBAS outage (Pullen & Gao 2012) caused by single chirp PPD. Vector tracking row has no chirp entry — corpus gap despite chirp being top real-world threat.

Pulsed DME / TACAN

Aeronautical radionavigation pulse pairs in L5/E5a band (960–1215 MHz). Not malicious — legitimate signals that interfere with civil GNSS L5.
Spectrum
Pulse pairs, 12 μs spacing, MHz BW
Source
DME/TACAN ground stations (legal aviation infra)
Best mitigation
Pulse blanking — Strong (Bastide 2004, Gao 2013)
Threshold
> 60% duty cycle → L5 parity with L1 C/A (Kim 2025)
Why it matters

Persistent over dense European airspace. CRPA row vs pulsed = Gap — spatial null steering untested against broadband DME signature.

4. Five Structural Gaps (Section 5.2)

  1. Pulsed (DME) vs CRPA spatial processing — entire row Gap.
  2. WB vs wavelet / STFT excision — no above-Sim entries.
  3. Chirp vs vector tracking — Gap despite chirp being dominant commercial threat.
  4. Any technique vs > 60% duty-cycle DME — Gao 2013 last flight test, > 10 years old.
  5. ML / DL vs real jammer signals — entire ML row stuck at Sim.
